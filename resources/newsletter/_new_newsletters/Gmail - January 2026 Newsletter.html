APA Council Update
 

CEO Artur Evans held a town hall for APA Council members on December 11, 2025, to provide an update of the organization. He noted that, although there have been some recent staff losses, the administrative structure is working well. He mentioned that APA has been strategically navigating a changing policy landscape yet has prioritized APA’s values and mission. In that regard, APA has sent over 113K messages to Congress citing psychological research/science on issues affecting individuals. They have an active APA/APASI resource center, which has provided resources, published policy insights, and been visited over 150K times. Dr. Evans cited a steadiness in APA membership, currently at 189K, expressing excitement at the growth in international members, and student membership at 62K. Dr. Evans mentioned APA’s outreach to the public through social media, podcasts, and media mentions, and ensuring that research/science information is packaged well in partnership with other organizations and social media platforms. APA’s advocacy staff have been active in getting psychology a “professional" designation to prevent cap/limits on student loans, have advocated for not having a 4-year limit on international student visas, and have continued to support diversity standards in the accreditation standards. In addition, APA has been working with the FTC to address mental health chatbots, noting that APA is attempting to stay current with the effect of AI. APA’s draft revisions to the Model Licensing Act will hopefully be out for public comment in early 2026, so please respond with comments as this is being revised.
 

Law and Human Behavior 2026 Editorial Fellow

Law and Human Behavior’s Editorial Fellowship Program seeks to elevate leadership opportunities for early-career psychologists from communities that have been historically underrepresented in research and publishing leadership roles. The Editorial Team of Law and Human Behavior is pleased to announce that Dr. Lindsay Healey was selected as the Law and Human Behavior Editorial Fellow for 2026. Dr. Healey is an early-career Scientist with the Integrated Forensic Program at the University of Ottawa Institute of Mental Health Research at The Royal in Ottawa, Canada. After completing a PhD in Forensic Psychology at Carleton University in 2023, she completed a postdoctoral fellowship in the Department of Psychological Science at the University of California, Irvine. Her program of research broadly focuses on the intersection of serious mental illness and the criminal justice system, with particular interest in advancing the rehabilitation and outcomes of individuals involved in the courts and the forensic mental health system. Dr. Healey will work closely with Dr. Jennifer Perillo, an Associate Editor for Law and Human Behavior, to develop editorial leadership skills. Dr. Healey will serve as an Editorial Fellow for calendar year 2026.

Call for Book Proposals

The American Psychology-Law Society invites proposals for new titles in its book series, which is now published by Oxford University Press. We publish authored and edited volumes on current issues at the intersection of psychology and law. Books are typically a state of the science synthesis of a body of existing research with implications for practice and/or policy. Authors and editors should have a substantial and recent track record of publication and/or practice in psychology or law, and at least one should hold a doctorate in psychology or closely related behavioral science. If you have a proposal for a new book in the series or you would simply like more information, please reach out to a member of the editorial team. We would be happy to discuss possible topics and fit for the series.

Request for proposals for Chapters and Essays

Monica K. Miller, David DeMatteo, and Daniel Krauss are editors of the book series

“Advances in Psychology and Law,” which seeks to enhance understanding of the connections between these disciplines, present “state of the science” summaries for current researchers, and provide a starting point for scholars wanting to delve into a new topic of study by reading an up-to-date review chapter or brief essay on that topic. All areas of psychology (e.g., developmental, social, cognitive, clinical, neuroscience) and law (e.g., criminal, civil, contracts, property, family) are appropriate for inclusion.  

    

Chapters and essays will be primarily reviews or meta-analyses. Although they might provide some data to illustrate a new phenomenon, they are not meant to be data-driven like a journal article. Review chapters and essays will be on particular topics relevant to psychology and law and will be of current interest to the field.  Chapters (and especially essays) will typically summarize research on one particular topic, rather than focusing on the entire body of research in an area (e.g., focusing on the Weapon Focus effect rather than the entire body of eyewitness memory). Chapters will be relatively long (30-40 pages d.s.), which will allow authors to provide in depth analysis and take calculated risks (e.g., engaging in informed speculation about future research or the consequences of particular legal policies). Essays will be shorter (approximately 10 pages d.s.) because they discuss a topic that is new and/or has less previous research to synthesize.

    

The book series has an editorial board of distinguished scholars who are experts in various areas in psychology and law. The editorial board will offer guidance about the development of topics and serve as expert reviewers of chapters and essays. The editors strive to produce one volume in the series approximately every 12-18 months.  

 

Overviews of the previous volumes can be viewed here to ensure topics have not been already included in the series and to see examples of chapters:  http://www.springer.com/series/11918?detailsPage=titles


Submit an approximately 300 word description and title of your proposed topic and indicate if you want to write an essay or chapter.  Specify both the legal component and the psychological component of your topic. Email submissions to Monica Miller at (mkmiller@unr.edu).
 

REMINDER: Webinars: Learning from Lived Experience

SAVE THE DATE: January 28, 2026 (3:00 EST / 12:00 PST)

These are part of the Presidential Initiative from Past-President Daniel Murrie.

 

The mission of AP-LS is “to enhance well-being, justice, and human rights through the application of psychological science and practice in legal contexts.” Our members often bring substantial clinical and scholarly expertise to this important mission. But another critical source of expertise in enhancing well-being, justice, and human rights is the expertise of persons with lived experience.

 

How might we better learn from those with lived experience? How might we collaborate on scholarship, policy, or clinical services in ways that honor their rights, dignity, and genuine expertise? To begin considering these questions, a series of webinars will share examples of fruitful collaborations that rely on experts with lived experience.  Our webinars in May of 2025 addressed lived experience in clinical services and training, as well as informing policy.  This session is more specific to research.

 

Incorporating Lived Experience into Psychology-Law Research

Join us for a 90-minute webinar on strategies for meaningfully incorporating the voices of people with lived experience (PWLE) in research related to the criminal legal system and substance use. Presenters will outline the benefits of engaging PWLE, introduce a practical framework grounded in community-based participatory research (CBPR), share real-world case studies, and offer actionable guidance for AP-LS researchers. Following a series of presentations, attendees will have the opportunity to engage in an open panel discussion. This session is designed for researchers across career stages and offers continuing education credit.

 

Please save the date if interested. A registration link will be forthcoming.
